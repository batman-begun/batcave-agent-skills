# SYNTHESIZE protocol — qualified, actionable intelligence

## Conditions

Rank options only after significant lanes and critical claims have been investigated and challenged, or explicitly declare that the output is a provisional snapshot. Use `quality-gates.md` to determine whether to present the result as qualified or partial.

## Synthesis steps

1. State user objective and any interpretation made; keep the user's hard constraints intact.
2. Give a *landscape map* (meaningful groups, not a superficial list of famous names). Highlight discoveries originating outside the user's seed context; don't pretend all such ideas are truly novel in the world.
3. Separate **evidence-backed findings**, **conditional inferences**, and **exploratory hypotheses** with visible labels when ambiguity matters. Link material facts to their evidence.
4. If recommending, apply a transparent criterion such as urgency, measurable pain, ability to reach buyers, existing substitutes, feasibility, risk, capital requirements, or cost-to-test. Weight criteria to the mission; do not invent market figures or precision scores.
5. For each serious idea, note strongest counterargument, assumption that could break it, and the cheapest discriminating validation: who to interview, what to measure, experiment design, costs to bound, and what result would kill the idea.
6. Expose researched lanes, uninvestigated lanes, source dates, conflicts, and reasons to stop. If tool access was limited, say what that prevents concluding.
7. Include suggested next actions in priority order; distinguish things Klein can do with present tools from human-only field research.

## Reporting modes

- **Quick:** direct answer + key limitations.
- **Standard:** discovery map + validated candidates + strong unknowns + counterevidence + test plan.
- **Deep:** discovery/claim ledger + cross-source comparison + caveats + prioritization + handoff summary if continuation is requested.

Never assert a market is empty merely because a search found no competitors; never treat qualitative anecdotes as market size; never equate citations with validation. Avoid filler, inflated certainty, or fake numerical completeness.
